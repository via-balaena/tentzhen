Project #6: p-bits

Research date for everything below: 2026-11-27, unless a line says otherwise.

Probabilistic bits, p-bits, wired together into a small thermodynamic computer: first as gateware on the bench's Colorlight i9, then fed by a physical noise source, then as a chip. Jon, 2026-11-27: "given my interest in microcontrollers and previous work with thermodynamic computing engineering/simulation in cortenforge, this is attractive to me." The plan came from Jon's Claude session the same day ("Where this came from", below). Nothing is built or ordered, and what each piece would do is unknown until it earns a grade.

What a p-bit computes

CortenForge's Gibbs sampler (cortenforge-sim-thermostat 0.9.0, src/gibbs.rs) states it. Spins σ ∈ {−1, +1} carry the energy

H(σ) = −Σ J_ij σ_i σ_j − Σ h_i σ_i

and each update sets one spin to +1 with probability "sigmoid(2·local_field_i / kT)", where spin i's local field is h_i plus the sum of J_ij σ_j over its neighbours (sweep, which updates the spins in turn). A p-bit is hardware that makes that update. CortenForge's exact_distribution (src/ising.rs) enumerates the distribution H gives, for up to 20 spins ("safety limit: 2^20 = 1M configurations"), and CortenForge checks its sampler against it (tests/gibbs_sampler.rs, "Validates the GibbsSampler against exact_distribution"). It is what a small network's measured histogram is checked against here.

Where it sits

  • CortenForge keeps the physics. Its thermodynamic computing line (via-balaena/CortenForge at 2a5cc70b, docs/thermo_computing/) builds "mechanical thermodynamic computing devices", and says the computing element "is not a transistor", since "Extropic builds p-bits out of transistors because they have a fab. CortenForge has cf-design and a 3D printer." Its physical p-bit, "D4", a spring-steel cantilever on a shaker read by Hall sensors, stays there.
  • Tentzhen builds the electronics: p-bits in logic on the i9, then in transistors, on an open-PDK shuttle or in our own process (fab.md, "The steps"). Tentzhen uses CortenForge as an end user, through its published crates (CLAUDE.md).
  • The i9 is on the bench already (lab/sourcing.toml), so the first steps need nothing bought.

What already exists

The sources are in prior-art.md, "p-bits and thermodynamic computing".

  • p-bits on FPGAs are well trodden, on Xilinx parts with Vivado. The sparse Ising machine (Aadit et al., arXiv 2110.02481) updates its p-bits in graph-coloured parallel, makes each one's randomness with "32-bit linear-feedback shift registers", maps tanh with "a lookup table", and flips with "a comparator", reaching "143.8 flips/ns" at 4,793 p-bits. pc-COP puts 2,048 fully connected p-bits on a ZCU104. Every FPGA p-bit design read that names its part is on a Xilinx or AMD FPGA, and quantinfo's open Verilog (MIT) needs "Vivado version: 2023.2 Enterprise".
  • Invertible logic is the classic demonstration. Camsari et al. (Phys. Rev. X 7, 031014, 2017) show "a 4-bit multiplier which in inverted mode functions as a factorizer", and that "p-bits need not be magnet-based". Borders et al. (Nature 573, 390, 2019) factored integers with p-bits made from magnetic tunnel junctions.
  • In Rust, ferrotherm (Apache-2.0) does thermodynamic computing in software, with an invertible multiplier its README calls "shipped, verified", and has layers of its own that emit FPGA bitstreams for the iCE40-HX8K and Artix-7. It has no ECP5 layer, and "Still not done: binding a p-bit fabric's LUTs to SLICE site pins".
  • On a shuttle, WobblyBits (Apache-2.0, Tiny Tapeout, SKY130) is "6 p-bits ... driven by hardware randomness from a bunch of on-chip ring oscillators", taken from neoTRNG. Its results so far are "the RTL results".
  • Thermodynamic computing companies: Extropic measured a transistor random-bit cell at about 10 MHz, using about 350 aJ per bit; the rest of its paper was "done using simulations of thermodynamic hardware". Its code, thrml, is Apache-2.0.
  • True random numbers from ring oscillators are open too: neoTRNG (BSD-3-Clause, implemented on an Artix-7, with no "online health monitoring"); a ring-oscillator TRNG for the ULX3S's ECP5, with no licence; and cryptoliteICE, whose RP2040 runs "SP 800-90B health tests" on an iCE40's ring oscillators.

What nothing we found does: p-bits on an ECP5, written in Amaranth and built with Yosys, nextpnr and Trellis; a p-bit core checked bit for bit against an open Rust model; and p-bits fed by an on-chip entropy source that runs SP 800-90B's health tests in the same gateware. That is the part built here. A p-bit made in a process older than 180 nm, or in an open fab, also turned up nowhere.

The steps

Each is a proposal, unknown until it earns a grade.

stepwhatpasses when
1A golden model, in Rust: p-bits in integer arithmetic, with the random-number generator and the sigmoid's table or threshold that the gateware will useon networks of up to 20 spins, its samples' histogram is within a distance of exact_distribution from cortenforge-sim-thermostat 0.9.0, the distance and the sample count set in the test before it first runs (tested)
2A p-bit core in Amaranthin simulation it draws exactly the golden model's samples, from the same seed (tested)
3p-bits on the i9a board run's histogram matches exact_distribution within the same distance, recorded as a measurement (measured)
4True randomness: ring oscillators on the ECP5, with SP 800-90B's health tests in the gateware, feeding the p-bitsthe health tests run on the board and the p-bits' histogram still matches (measured); what the source's entropy is stays unknown until it is estimated
5A p-bit chip, on a shuttle and then in our own processits histogram matches (measured); a shuttle costs money, which is Jon's decision (fab.md, "The steps")
  • The first networks are small enough to enumerate, as CortenForge's exact_distribution requires: a ferromagnetic ring, a max-cut on K3,3 as WobblyBits runs, and an invertible AND gate. A multiplier run backward is the showcase after those; ferrotherm's 4×4 multiplier takes 176 spins, and whether that many fit the i9 beside the rest is unknown.
  • The random-number generator for steps 1–3 is not chosen. LFSRs (the sparse Ising machine, pc-COP) and Xoshiro (Nikhar et al.) are what the FPGA designs read use; the golden model fixes whichever is chosen.
  • The noise source of step 4 is not claimed fit for keys. SP 800-90B asks that its health tests "shall include both continuous and start-up tests", run "before any conditioning is done", and that on a persistent failure "the entropy source shall not produce any outputs".
  • The fab's verifier uses step 4's health tests too, on a different source: the RP2350's ring oscillator, before it hashes each challenge (fab.md, "Security"). So the tests are written once more, in Rust, as the model the gateware's tests are checked against, and the verifier runs that model.
  • Loading the i9 follows the lab-target rules, as project #3's board runs do (littlebit.md). Steps 1 to 4 handle no chemicals, heat, high voltage, vacuum or pressure; step 5's own process is the fab's, under its safety rules (fab.md, "Safety first").
  • Later, a dev kit: the i9, its extension board, the core and the golden model, published together, as the session recommends ("Where this came from").

Where this came from

Jon's Claude session of 2026-11-27, whose summary Jon brought the same day. It set out a thermodynamic chip as the strongest candidate for a young fab, a path ("your sims as the reference model, then p-bits on an FPGA, then fabbed devices using real physical noise"), its applications, and a dev kit with a built-in RNG as the launch. Kept: the path, the applications and the dev kit. Changed here:

  • The path gains a golden model and a noise step. A Rust model bit-exact with the gateware stands between CortenForge's sampler and the i9. CortenForge's sampler draws from a ChaCha8 generator in floating point (src/gibbs.rs), which the gateware is not planned to repeat, so the two are compared by their distributions. True randomness is its own step, with health tests.
  • "It suits a young fab especially well, because noisy, imperfect devices are a feature rather than a defect": kept as a question, unknown until a device shows it. The oldest process a p-bit chip was found in is 180 nm, with RRAM.
  • "True random number generation (simplest and most sellable)": kept as step 4; "most sellable" is left out, with no source.
  • The chips before it (process test chips, a RISC-V microcontroller, hardwired classifiers, a neural physics surrogate, analog compute-in-memory) are not taken up here; fab.md's own steps stand.
  • Left out, with no source found: "Nvidia reportedly cutting gaming GPU supply over memory shortages". The physics card and the neural-physics plugin are CortenForge's, or a product's, not Tentzhen's. The resistor scanner fits project #5's intake as a parts sorter, once the stores exist.
